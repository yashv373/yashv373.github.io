Comparative Analysis of Sense Amplifier Circuits for Hybrid CMOS-MTJ CIM Architecture

Yashvardhan Singh, Siddharth Penumatsa, and Prashanth Barla

IEEE Journal on Exploratory Solid-State Computational Devices and Circuits (JXCDC), 2026

STT-MTJ memory is a strong fit for compute-in-memory, but reading it reliably is hard. We benchmark six current-mode sense amplifiers (PCSA, IPCSA, SPCSA, RESPCSA, REDSA, HRSA) on power, sensing margin, read delay and transistor count, and run 1000-run Monte Carlo simulations to find each one's read error rate under process and device variation. The paper works as a guide to which sense amplifier to choose for which CIM application.

Exploring Dopant Choice in HfO2 FE Layer for Negative Capacitance Junctionless FinFET Based Sensors through Sentaurus TCAD

Yashvardhan Singh and Megh Giri

Research Square (Springer Nature) preprint, 2025

We build a negative-capacitance junctionless FinFET sensor in Sentaurus TCAD and try six other dopants in its HfO2 ferroelectric layer instead of the usual zirconium. The dopant sets how strong the negative-capacitance effect is, which lets the transistor run at very low voltages and pick up very small changes.

Research Square / GitHub

Spintronic Cryptography: CMOS-MTJ Hybrid Circuits for Secure Storage Infrastructure

Yashvardhan Singh

SSRN (Elsevier) preprint, 2025

Proposes swapping the volatile pipeline registers in SHA-256 hashing hardware for non-volatile CMOS-MTJ storage, so the pipeline can power off without leaking energy. I simulated a 32-bit hybrid barrel shifter (about 5,000 transistors) in Cadence Virtuoso and wrote up where today's verification flows run out of steam.

SSRN

Semicustom Frontend VLSI Design and Analysis of a 32-bit Brent-Kung Adder in Cadence Suite

Yashvardhan Singh

arXiv preprint, 2025

A semicustom 90 nm design of a 32-bit Brent-Kung adder in the Cadence suite, benchmarked against a range of other adders, including Kogge-Stone and Han-Carlson, on delay and other trade-offs. Other researchers have since cited it in their adder and multiplier work.

ASCON-128a crypto accelerator

May – Sep 2026

IEEE SSCS PICO Chipathon 2026 · with RS Tarun (IIIT Bangalore) and Lakshmi Iyer (IIT Bombay)

A plug-and-play encryption IP for open-source SoCs: a 320-bit ASCON-128a core (NIST SP 800-232) behind an AXI4-Lite interface.

  • RTL to GDSII on GF180MCU with LibreLane: 0.92 mm², 16 MHz across all corners, 18 mW, DRC/LVS clean.
