I'm Yashvardhan Singh, a digital integrated circuit design engineer.

I work on hardware acceleration, digital ASIC design, open-source silicon, machine learning for EDA, and post-CMOS devices like spintronics (CMOS-MTJ). I like taking designs all the way from RTL to GDSII. I also co-founded ChipMIT, India's first student-run chip design club.

Exploring Dopant Choice in HfO2 FE Layer for Negative Capacitance Junctionless FinFET Based Sensors through Sentaurus TCAD Preprint

Yashvardhan Singh and Megh Giri

Research Square (Springer Nature) preprint, 2025

We build a negative-capacitance junctionless FinFET sensor in Sentaurus TCAD and try six other dopants in its HfO2 ferroelectric layer instead of the usual zirconium.

Spintronic Cryptography: CMOS-MTJ Hybrid Circuits for Secure Storage Infrastructure Preprint

Yashvardhan Singh

SSRN (Elsevier) preprint, 2025

Proposes swapping the volatile pipeline registers in SHA-256 hashing hardware for non-volatile CMOS-MTJ storage, so the pipeline can power off without leaking energy.

Projects

ASCON-128a crypto accelerator

May – Sep 2026

IEEE SSCS PICO Chipathon 2026 · with RS Tarun (IIIT Bangalore) and Lakshmi Iyer (IIT Bombay)

A plug-and-play encryption IP for open-source SoCs: a 320-bit ASCON-128a core (NIST SP 800-232) behind an AXI4-Lite interface.

  • RTL to GDSII on GF180MCU with LibreLane: 0.92 mm², 16 MHz across all corners, 18 mW, DRC/LVS clean.
  • Verified with NIST test vectors, a Cocotb model and gate-level sims; packaged as IP-XACT.
  • 2nd in the proposal round and approved for tapeout.

GitHub

Project Kalki

Sep 2025 – Feb 2026

ChipMIT's flagship project · with Megh Giri

A 4×4 systolic array (16 MAC units) for NPU-style matrix math, taken from FPGA exploration to a finished SkyWater 130 nm layout.

  • OpenLane flow: 400 × 400 µm die, 2.385 mW at 100 MHz, 1.49 pJ per MAC.
  • Won Silicon Sprint 2025; Special Mention at SYNCHRON'25.

GitHub / Silicon Sprint build / Demo video

32-bit Brent-Kung adder

Jan – Mar 2025

VLSI Design Lab

Semicustom 90 nm Cadence design, benchmarked against a range of other adders, including ripple-carry, carry-lookahead, Kogge-Stone and Han-Carlson: 3.78 ns, 43 µW, 1224 µm². Became a cited arXiv preprint.
