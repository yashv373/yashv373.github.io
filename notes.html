05 Timing

Setup and hold time

Updated 11 Oct 2026  timingsta

Every flip-flop needs its input D to sit still for a short time around the clock edge.

  • Setup time ($t_{su}$): how long D must be stable before the edge.
  • Hold time ($t_h$): how long D must stay stable after the edge.

Break either one and the flop can go metastable, which is also the core problem in Clock domain crossing.

Setup check

Data launched on one clock edge must arrive before the next edge, with setup time to spare:

$$t_{cq} + t_{logic,max} + t_{su} \le T_{clk} + t_{skew}$$

If this fails, you can slow the clock, shorten the logic path, or split it with a pipeline stage.

Hold check

New data must not arrive too early and overwrite the value being captured on the same edge:

$$t_{cq} + t_{logic,min} \ge t_h + t_{skew}$$

Hold does not depend on the clock period, so slowing the clock never fixes a hold violation. The usual fix is adding delay (buffers) on the short path.

Here $t_{skew}$ = capture clock arrival time minus launch clock arrival time.

03 RTL Design › Clocking

Clock domain crossing

Updated 11 Oct 2026  cdcmetastability

A clock domain crossing (CDC) happens when a signal made on one clock is read by flops on a different, unrelated clock. The two clocks' edges have no fixed relationship, so sometimes the receiving flop samples the signal right while it changes. That breaks Setup and hold time and the flop goes metastable.

02 Architecture › Accelerators

Systolic arrays

Updated 11 Oct 2026  ai hardware

A systolic array is a grid of small, identical processing elements (PEs). Each PE does a multiply-accumulate (MAC) and passes data to its neighbours every clock cycle, like a heartbeat pumping data through the grid. That's where the name comes from. The original roots stem from H T Kung's paper on Systolics, who is the same person who was involved in Brent-Kung adders.

I built a 4×4 one in Project Kalki.
